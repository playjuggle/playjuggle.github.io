# Puzzle Quality Standard

This standard is binding for every Juggle puzzle. Read it before authoring or reviewing any puzzle. Structural validation does not replace this editorial review.

## Hard editorial requirements

1. **No hypernyms.** No answer may be a category, class, or container that includes another answer in the same puzzle. If one answer is an example of another, the puzzle is invalid.
2. **Centrality.** Every primary answer must be something a reasonable person would name within the first fifteen items if asked to list things belonging to the theme. Peripheral or merely technically defensible words are invalid.
3. **Keystone final.** The final answer must be a keystone: the place, event, container, outcome, or summarizing noun that the five primaries belong to, produce, or assemble into. It must deliver a payoff the player recognizes on sight. A sixth item of the same kind as the primaries is invalid, as is a word unrelated to the theme.
4. **No filler nouns.** PAPERS, THINGS, ITEMS, STUFF, PIECES, OBJECT, and similar contentless generics are banned as answers.
5. **Theme specificity.** Consider the six words without their theme. If they could plausibly belong to three or more unrelated themes, the theme is too vague and the puzzle is invalid.
6. **Mandatory weakest-link critique.** Before validating each puzzle, identify its single weakest answer and write one sentence explaining why it stays. If that justification is not convincing on its own terms, replace the word and repeat the critique. Asking whether a puzzle is acceptable is not a check; naming its weakest element is required.

## Motivating failure

The puzzle themed **Home Office** with primaries LAPTOP, TABLET, DEVICE, PAPERS, CHECKS and final POCKET passed the old rubric and validator, but was unacceptable: DEVICE contains LAPTOP and TABLET; POCKET is not a home-office item and was the final; PAPERS is filler; CHECKS is peripheral.

## Structural and audience requirements

- One clear, natural, specific theme.
- Five distinct six-letter primary answers and one six-letter final answer.
- Exactly six marked letters across the five primaries; in word order, those letters must anagram to the final answer.
- Use familiar contemporary English for a broad US adult audience.
- No proper nouns, trademarks, abbreviations, jargon, very rare words, questionable spellings, or inflections strained to reach six letters.
- Nothing insensitive, political, adult, or likely to age badly.

Passing a structural validator does not establish editorial quality. Apply every hard editorial requirement and record the weakest-link sentence for every puzzle in its quality-control record.
